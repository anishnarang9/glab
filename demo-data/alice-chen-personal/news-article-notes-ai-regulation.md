# Article Notes: EU AI Act Implementation — What it Means for Research

Read a long piece in MIT Tech Review about how the EU AI Act is starting to hit academic labs. Some notes:

## What I took away
- High-risk AI systems (including medical-adjacent stuff like BCIs) need conformity assessments
- Research labs get an exemption but it's narrower than people think — anything with human subjects is murky
- The "general purpose AI" provisions are still being interpreted and are confusing everyone

## Is this relevant to us?
Possibly. If Clara's BCI work ever gets commercialized (which is the whole point), it would definitely fall under high-risk. But for now we're firmly in basic research exemption territory.

The fMRI decoding work is further from clinical application so probably fine.

The article mentioned brain-computer interfaces specifically as a "sensitive AI application." I've been in this field long enough that it still surprises me when journalists treat our work as scary. To us it's just math and signal processing.
